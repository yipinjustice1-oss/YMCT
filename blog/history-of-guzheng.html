古筝的两千五百年历史 The 2,500-Year History of the Guzheng

古筝是中国最古老的弹拨乐器之一，其历史可追溯至战国时期（约公元前5世纪），距今已有超过两千五百年。了解它的演变，能帮助学习者更深刻地体会这件乐器承载的文化重量。 The guzheng is one of China's oldest plucked string instruments, with roots tracing back to the Warring States period (around the 5th century BCE) — over 2,500 years ago. Understanding its evolution helps students appreciate the cultural weight this instrument carries.

起源：从「瑟」到「筝」Origins: From the "Se" to the "Zheng"

古筝的前身可追溯到更早的「瑟」，一种拥有25根弦的大型乐器。据历史记载，秦国（今陕西一带）逐渐发展出弦数较少、更便于携带演奏的「筝」，因此古筝也被称为「秦筝」。关于「筝」字由来，民间流传着兄弟分瑟相争的传说，虽未必属实，却生动反映了这件乐器名称的文化渊源。 The guzheng's predecessor is the "se," an earlier, larger 25-string instrument. Historical records suggest that the state of Qin (in today's Shaanxi region) gradually developed a version with fewer strings that was easier to carry and play — the "zheng," which is why the guzheng is also called the "Qin zheng." Folklore explains the character "zheng" (争, meaning "to contend") through a legend of brothers fighting over a se, a story that may be more myth than fact, but vividly reflects the instrument's cultural roots.

弦数的演变：从12弦到21弦The Evolution of String Count: From 12 to 21

汉唐时期的古筝多为12至13弦，音域相对有限。20世纪中期，音乐家们对古筝进行了重大改革，将弦数增加至21根，并采用尼龙缠弦取代传统丝弦，大幅拓宽了音域与表现力。这一改革后的21弦古筝，正是我们今天最常见的标准形制。 During the Han and Tang dynasties, the guzheng typically had 12 to 13 strings with a relatively limited range. In the mid-20th century, musicians undertook major reforms, expanding the instrument to 21 strings and replacing traditional silk strings with nylon-wound ones, greatly widening its range and expressive power. This reformed 21-string guzheng is the standard form most commonly played today.

从宫廷雅乐到现代舞台From Court Music to the Modern Stage

古筝曾是宫廷雅乐与文人雅集的重要乐器，象征高雅的艺术修养。如今，它不仅活跃于传统民乐团与考级舞台，也频繁出现在电影配乐、流行音乐甚至跨界演出中，持续以新的姿态走向世界。 The guzheng was once a central instrument in court music and scholarly gatherings, symbolizing refined artistic cultivation. Today, it thrives not only in traditional ensembles and grading exams but also appears frequently in film scores, popular music, and cross-genre performances — continuously reaching new audiences around the world.
